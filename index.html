How I work

Three layers, and I have worked all three

Most analyst roles sit in one of these. The reason I can fix a number end to end is that I have built the system that generates it, the pipeline that moves it, and the dashboard that reports it.

01

Instrument the revenue motion

The rules that decide what a deal costs, who approves it, and when it becomes an order. Configuration work, not commentary on it.

  • Salesforce CPQ: product and price rules, bundles, option constraints
  • Discount approval matrices, thresholds, escalation paths
  • Weighted forecast models from win rates and stage velocity
  • Pipeline hygiene against stage exit criteria and validation rules
  • Quote-to-order and quote-to-cash automation
02

Build the data layer under it

Getting CRM, billing, and vendor systems to agree. This is where most bad numbers are actually born.

  • Salesforce to Snowflake ELT pipelines, with results written back
  • dbt models for segment-specific business logic
  • SQL identity resolution and deduplication at million-record scale
  • Cross-system reconciliation: provisioned vs billed, vendor vs CRM
  • Python and Gemini API for the parts rules cannot resolve
03

Put the numbers in front of people

A correct number nobody looks at changes nothing. Self-serve for the people doing the work, a narrative for the people funding it.

  • Tableau, Power BI, and Looker KPI dashboards and scorecards
  • Self-serve views for reps and managers, not just leadership
  • Monthly and weekly business review materials, and the follow-through
  • A/B tests and experiment readouts stakeholders can act on
  • Metric definitions written down so two teams stop disagreeing
